360 Bike Capture Guide

For street surveys by bike with the Insta360 ONE RS 1-Inch 360. Every rule comes from our two UC campus rides on 29 Sep (clips 065 and 066). The goal is a 3DGS model where both buildings and road are sharp.

6K 360 · 30 fpsCamera 2 m highShutter 1/1000 sISO auto ≤ 800One frame every 0.6 m
1

Point the two lenses at the sides

Each lens is sharpest straight ahead of it. The join between the two lenses (the stitch seam) is the weakest part. Put buildings in the sharp zone and the seams on the road ahead and behind.

In front of a lens: sharpestStitch seam: near objects misalign or ghostLens
2

Reading a 360 frame: what is sharp and what misaligns

Clip 065 return, second 35. Green is where each lens points. The orange dashed lines are the seams. The orange bands are where both lenses overlap.

  • Near things (road, posts, you and the bike within 1 to 3 m) misalign or ghost when they cross a seam. The two lenses sit a few centimetres apart, so they see them from slightly different angles.
  • Far buildings across a seam look fine. Both lenses see them almost the same way.
  • The top and bottom look stretched because of the 360 projection. It goes back to normal when the frame is cut into flat views.
3

Camera 2 m high, upright, pole straight below

Side view. Level views only reach 45° down, so the ground within 2 m of the camera is never seen. Add a view tilted 30° down and the ground is covered from 0.5 m to 7.5 m.

  • Lane markings are about 10 to 15 cm wide, so the road within 10 m of your path is clear. Beyond 20 m it will be blurry.
  • Keep the pole straight below the camera, in line with it, so the invisible selfie stick effect works.

  • The cm values are the size of one pixel on the ground. Smaller is sharper. A higher camera looks at the ground less steeply, so the road 5 to 10 m away gets sharper. Close by it is about the same.
  • But a higher pole sways more in the wind. Watch for branches, signs and power lines. On a bike use 2 to 2.5 m. On a car use about 3 m (roof plus a pole of about 1.5 m).
  • The higher the camera, the less of you shows in the down views. I set the view angles when I cut the frames. In the field, just keep the height fixed.
4

Speed decides how many frames to take per second

Frames about 0.6 m apart give near objects enough overlap. Formula: fps = speed (m/s) ÷ 0.6. The video is 30 fps, so the most you can take is 10 frames per second.

  • Less blur is better. Under 0.5 cm (smaller than one pixel on the nearest ground) you can't see it. From 0.5 to 1 cm only the road within 2 m of the camera gets a bit soft. Over 1 cm the road and markings get clearly blurry.
  • Ride at 5 to 10 km/h. Clip 066 was ridden at about 4 km/h and came out about 50% sharper than 065 (about 7 km/h, handheld).
  • Set the shutter manually to 1/1000 s. In low light drop to 1/500. The blur then doubles, so slow down too.
  • Try not to stop. Stopped frames are removed automatically, but too many waste data.
5

Pick an overcast day or midday for short shadows

Your shadow moves with you and falls right where the down views are sharpest. The lower the sun, the longer the shadow. For a person plus camera 2 m tall:

  • Overcast is best: no hard shadows and even road colour. Midday is next best: the shadow is short and right under you, so the 30° down views mostly miss it.
  • Before you go, check light and wind in Shoot Check and avoid hours marked "Clear sky with low sun".
6

Wind: check both mean wind and gusts

Wind moves leaves (trees go blurry in the model) and sways a pole taller than 2 m. Check the hourly forecast in Shoot Check. Limits (the forecast is wind at 10 m above ground):

OK to shootTake careDon't shoot
Mean windBelow 20 km/h20 to 30 km/hAbove 30 km/h
GustsBelow 30 km/h30 to 45 km/hAbove 45 km/h
  • Below 20 km/h: leaves move, branches stay still. 20 to 30 km/h: small branches sway, so check the footage. Above 30 km/h: small trees sway and gusts shake the camera.
  • At 2 to 3 m the real wind is usually lower than the 10 m forecast, so these limits are on the safe side. The taller the pole, the more gusts matter.
  • These are provisional values. I will adjust them with campus tests and a handheld anemometer. Tonga has trade winds and is windy most days, so it needs its own limits (see rule 7).

Tonga: which months suit capture

Past weather for Nuku'alofa, 2021 to 2025, daytime 08:00 to 17:00, checked with the Shoot Check rules (wind, gusts, rain).

  • June to September is best, June most of all: the most shootable hours and the least rain (about 18 to 20%). The planned August trip is a reasonable choice.
  • November to March is the wet and cyclone season, with rain in 37 to 47% of daytime hours. This is the worst time.
  • Daytime wind is 18 to 24 km/h all year (trade winds) and hardly changes through the day. So rain and cyclones decide the month, not wind.
  • After a disaster you can't pick the weather. Use a response mode: stay in only when mean wind is over 40 km/h, gusts are over 60 km/h, or there is heavy rain or a cyclone warning. Shoot at all other times and note the weather. That leaves 81 to 95% of daytime hours in every month.
  • The data is ERA5 reanalysis. Its grid point is about 8 km from town and may sit over the sea, so wind may read high. ERA5 also tends to overcount light rain. The month-to-month pattern is still reliable.
8

Driving (Tonga)

Driving at 10 to 15 km/h gives the same sharpness as a bike at that speed ("Clear" to "Acceptable" in rule 4). A car is steadier, so it may even be sharper.

  • Mount the camera on a pole in the middle of the roof, about 3 m high in total, upright, with the lenses facing the sides of the road.
  • The roof blocks the view straight down, so use down views of about 20 to 25° to avoid it. The road is then covered from about 2 m out.
  • At 10 km/h take 5 frames per second, at 15 km/h take 7. Shutter 1/1000 s.
  • Engine vibration travels up the pole. Use a suction or roof-rack mount and keep the joints tight.
  • Driving this slowly holds up traffic. Pick quiet times, use hazard lights, and have someone else drive.
9

Ride the same road both ways, and don't turn the camera

With one pass the road is only seen from one direction, and the model streaks along the riding direction. With one pass each way, the same road is seen from both directions.

  • In 065 the camera was turned partway: you were behind it at first and in front of it later. I had to change the views by segment. Next time keep the same grip for the whole ride.
  • On the way back you can ride the other side of the road, so the road is seen from more angles.
10

Field steps

Before you go

  1. Shoot Check: light and rain OK, mean wind below 20 km/h, gusts below 30 km/h
  2. Camera set to 6K 360, 30 fps
  3. Shutter manual 1/1000 s, ISO auto up to 800
  4. Clean the lenses. Mount on an upright pole 2 m high
  5. Lenses facing the sides. Note which side of the camera you are on

While riding

  1. Start Strava (GPS) first, then start recording
  2. Ride at a steady speed, see rule 4
  3. Don't turn the camera or stop recording
  4. Ride the same road both ways
  5. Stop as little as possible, avoid busy times

After the ride

  1. Export as 360° video in Insta360 Studio with FlowState and auto horizon on
  2. Name files as clip_segment_start-end, for example 065_R_1115-1305
  3. Never use the same name as the original file
  4. Time zero of the video is the mp4 creation_time. Use it to line up the GPS

Based on: two UC campus rides on 29 Sep, 065 (handheld, about 15:30, sunny) and 066 (backpack, about 17:14, sunny), with their COLMAP registration, GPS alignment error and 3DGS results. Ground resolution assumes 1472-pixel, 90° views.
